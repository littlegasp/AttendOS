# Performance at larger company sizes (measured 19 Nov 2026)

Synthetic companies generated by `supabase/perf/generate.sql` (people across 8 departments and 3 offices,
60 days of check-ins/outs at ~90% attendance), timed with `supabase/perf/timings.sql` as the company's HR
user, PostgreSQL 16 on this sandbox (single small machine; hosted Supabase will differ).

| Call (screen) | 100 people | 300 people | 600 people |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dashboard_summary` (Dashboard) | 0.37 s | 0.78 s | 1.57 s |
| `attendance_register`, one day (Register → Daily) | 0.17 s | 0.36 s | 0.78 s |
| `attendance_register`, 7 days | 0.74 s | 2.1 s | 3.9 s |
| `attendance_register`, 30 days (Monthly / Employee detail) | 3.2 s | 8.2 s | 16.5 s |
| `report_people`, 30 days (Reports, Analytics) | 3.2 s | 8.2 s | 16.6 s |
| `report_trend`, 30 days (Analytics, Daily report) | 3.3 s | 8.6 s | 16.6 s |
| `my_today` (My Attendance) | 4 ms | 5 ms | 6 ms |
| Employee directory page (25) | 5 ms | 15 ms | 30 ms |

**Why:** the register, reports and dashboard work out each person's day live (`day_for`: roster, shift,
holidays, leave, events) — about 0.9 ms per person-day, growing linearly with people × days.
Check-in/out and the personal screens don't depend on company size.

**Hard limits to know before go-live**
- Supabase sets a **statement timeout of 8 seconds** for signed-in API calls by default. At ~300 people a 30-day
  report or monthly register is already at that limit; at 600 it would fail.
- The monthly register returns at most 5,000 rows (about 160 people × 31 days); beyond that the screen says to
  narrow the filters.
- Reports are limited to 93 days per request.

**Recommendation**
- Pilot with one company of **up to ~100 people**: every screen answers in about 3 s or less.
- Before companies of ~150+ people, add a **daily summary table** (`attendance_days`: one row per person per
  day with status, times, minutes) kept up to date by the check-in/out, correction, leave, roster and holiday
  functions, plus a nightly job (Supabase cron) that recomputes yesterday and the coming week. The register,
  reports, analytics and dashboard then read rows instead of recalculating — expected well under a second for
  thousands of people. Today's row stays live.
- Also needed then: paging the monthly grid by people, and running exports in pages.
